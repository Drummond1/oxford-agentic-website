People ask what time of year executive AI cohorts begin, and the honest answer is that the question has aged out of usefulness. It made sense when executive education followed the university calendar and you waited for an autumn intake. Most AI programmes no longer work that way.

## What actually varies now

Online programmes repeat. Oxford's own generative and agentic AI programme runs online across four weeks and lists two remaining start dates for 2026, on 16 September and 11 November, rather than a single autumn intake (checked on its own page, 31 August 2026). That is how most online executive AI education behaves now, because the subject moves too quickly to teach annually - a curriculum written for last September is visibly dated by spring.

In-person days are the exception, and they are scheduled one date at a time rather than in a season. Ours are set individually: the next Oxford Agentic Bootcamp runs on 16 September at Worcester College, and the Second Brain Bootcamp on 21 October at the Pitt Rivers Museum. Both that programme and our own September day happen to begin on 16 September, which makes that particular choice a clean one: four weeks online, or one day in a room with your own work in front of you.

So the practical advice is dull but true: check the provider's page rather than waiting for a season that no longer exists. If you are looking at an online programme and the next cohort is two months out, there is very likely another one after it.

## The timing question worth asking instead

Since availability has stopped being the constraint, the useful question is where you are rather than what month it is.

Most people arrive at this having done the reading. They can hold their own on agentic AI in a board discussion and could not build one if you asked them to tomorrow. That gap is not fixed by finding a better course to understand it with. It is fixed by building something.

The sequence that works is unglamorous:

- **Understand the frame first, if you do not have it.** The economics, the governance questions, where this fits your organisation. A [programme or a structured course](/guides/ai-bootcamp-vs-course-vs-exec-education/) is a reasonable way to get it, and it ages well.
- **Then build one thing.** Not a second course. One workflow, against a task from your own week, that still runs on Monday.
- **Then bring it back.** The step almost everyone skips, and the one that decides whether any of it survives contact with the organisation.

## The mistake in waiting

There is a version of this where you wait for the right cohort, the quieter quarter, the moment the diary clears. It rarely arrives, and the cost of waiting is not neutral.

The tools improve every month. The ability to build with them does not improve on its own, and the distance between the two is what widens. Someone who understood agentic AI well in March and has still built nothing understands it slightly less well now, because the thing they understood has moved.

If you have already finished a programme, the next step is not another one - [that gap has its own page](/guides/after-the-ai-executive-programme/). If you have not, you can honestly do either first. What does not work is doing the understanding twice.

## What to do between cohorts

If the date you want is weeks away, the gap is not dead time. Pick the task you would bring. It should be something you already do by hand most weeks, boring enough that nobody would miss it, with a recognisable before and after - [choosing it well](/guides/how-to-choose-your-first-agentic-task/) does more for the day than any amount of preparatory reading.

Arriving with that decided is the single biggest difference between people who leave with something running and people who leave with notes.
