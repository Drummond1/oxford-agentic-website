If you have been asked to sort out AI training, you will have noticed that almost every provider sells the same shape: they come to you, for a day, for your team. It is a good format. It is also the only one most of them offer, which means the honest comparison with the alternative rarely gets written.

The alternative is sending two or three people to an open cohort and letting what they learn spread from there. Neither is better. They are good at different things, and the choice is usually decided by something other than price.

## What an in-house day is good at

- **Everyone ends up with the same way of working.** If the problem is that six people each invented their own approach, a shared room on a shared day fixes it in a way that sending one person never will.
- **Your own systems, in the room.** Real data, real permissions, real constraints, and whoever administers your tools sitting two seats away when something needs approving.
- **Confidential work stays inside.** Some tasks cannot be discussed in front of strangers, and that settles it on its own.
- **No travel, and one date to agree.** For a team of twelve, that is not a small thing.

## What a cohort is good at

- **Nobody has to organise it.** No minimum headcount, no venue, no scoping call. Someone books a seat.
- **The other people in the room.** This is the part that does not survive being brought in-house. You hear five other ways to approach your problem, from people with no stake in your politics, and some of them are better than yours.
- **It suits small numbers honestly.** Below about four people, a private day is usually poor value, and most providers will still happily sell you one.
- **Being out of the building.** A day at your own desk is a day where people get pulled into their normal work at eleven o'clock.

## The question that settles it

Ask what you are actually buying: **alignment, or capability?**

If the goal is that a team stops doing something inconsistently, you are buying alignment, and alignment is made in a room together. If the goal is that two or three capable people come back able to build things and show others, you are buying capability, and capability travels home in individuals.

A second test, if the first is close: would the day still be worth it if only half the invited list turned up? For a cohort the answer is yes, because each person's value is their own. For an in-house day it is usually no, which tells you how much of its value depends on getting everyone in the room on one date.
