These labels get used interchangeably, but they describe genuinely different things. The cleanest way to choose is to ask what you want to be holding when it ends.

## Executive education: a frame

University executive programmes - the diplomas, the strategy modules - are good at what they are for: the landscape, the economics, the governance questions, a peer network, a credential. If you need to set direction, allocate budget or speak credibly about AI at board level, this is the right instrument.

Some now include a build exercise as well, and the better ones are worth doing. What they rarely leave you with is your own work running afterwards: a guided build on a sample case is a different thing from the task that was actually on your desk on Monday. That is not a flaw; it is not the job. Many of the people in our rooms have completed exactly this kind of programme and arrive saying some version of the same thing: I understand it - I have just never built anything.

## An online course: knowledge, at your pace

Online courses are the most flexible and the most affordable route, and the good ones teach real material. They suit people who want depth on their own schedule, and they are unbeatable for reference - you can return to a module the week you need it.

Their weakness is completion, and more precisely application. Watching someone build is not building. The friction that stops people is rarely the concept; it is the first stuck moment at their own desk, with no one to unstick them. That is where most course journeys quietly end.

## A bootcamp: a result

A bootcamp - at least as we mean it - is a short, facilitated stretch of time with one measure of success: you leave with something working. The format exists to remove the two things that stall self-directed learning: the setup friction, because someone walks the room, and the deferral, because a date in the diary with other people in the room is a commitment kept.

The trade-off is scope. A day cannot cover a landscape; it covers one pattern, properly, applied to one real task from your own work. Our view is that this trade is the right one for operators - the pattern transfers, and the working result is what turns understanding into habit.

## Choosing between them

- **You need to set strategy or govern AI use** - executive education.
- **You want broad knowledge on your own schedule** - an online course.
- **You want something built and running against your own work** - a bootcamp.
- **You have already done the first two** - that is the most common person in our room, and the gap they describe is exactly what a bootcamp closes.

They compound rather than compete: the frame from a programme, the depth from a course and a working result from a day of building are worth more together than any one alone.
